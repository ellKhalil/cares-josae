ASSESSMENT OF TRAINING NEEDS AMONG SMALLHOLDER FARMERS IN NINGI LOCAL GOVERNMENT AREA OF BAUCHI STATE NIGERIA

*1Abdullahi, M., 1Salisu, A.H., 1Idris, H., and 1Abdullahi, B.I.
Download Manuscript (82.0 KB)
Abstract: The study assessed agricultural extension training programs among smallholder farmers in Ningi local government area, Bauchi State, Nigeria. A multi-stage sampling technique was used in selecting 120 respondents for the study. Data were collected with aid of structured questionnaire and analyzed using both descriptive and inferential statistics. The result reveals that, majority of the respondents (71.1%) were male with (35.8%) of the respondent fell between 46-55 years and (30.8%) had never been to school. The result also shows that almost half of the famers (49.2%) owned about 2- 4 ha. with majority of them (73.5%) had household size ranges from 1-18. The result of the t-test shows that more men attended extension training as compared to women, however on the issue of farmers attendance at extension training in relation to gender there was no significance difference between males and females at (P<0.05) level. The result of the analysis of variances also, indicated that there were no significance differences between different levels of education and the farmers’ perception of extension service in the study area. The result of the logistic regression analysis shows that the training need of farmers were significantly (P<0.05) influenced by marital status, farming experience, and number of attendances to extension training. Major constraints were improper planning of extension training programs, poor transportation network and lack of motivation. The study recommends that extension agents should have a known schedule for training so as to help farmers allocate time for such training.

Estimation of Crop Water Requirement for Different Crops at Dambatta Thomas Dam Command Area, Kano State, Nigeria

1I.A Adam and 2Abdulazeez Hudu Wudil
Download Manuscript (539.0 KB)
Abstract: Accurate estimation of crop water requirements (ETc) of any crop is essentially required for irrigation scheduling and water management. This work evaluated the water requirement of four irrigated crops (Groundnut, Maize, Onion and Tomato) using Hargreaves Equation and FAO-56 guidelines to compute the reference and potential evapotranspiration of the crops respectively. The crop water requirements estimated were compared with the water applied and the actual duration of crops in the field as per the practices followed by the farmers. The results showed that the crops water requirements for groundnut, maize, onion and tomato based on FAO-56 guideline are 602mm, 715mm, 1029mm, and 661mm, while the average water applied are 559mm, 577mm, 819mm, and 557mm respectively. The crops were also found to be harvested far earlier than recommended by FAO-56 guideline

*1Ashiru, R. M., 2Iliyasu, K. A., 3Madu, A., 4Sariyyu, S., 5Afolabi, H.O., 6Babandi, B., 6Ubayi, K. M., 7Ado, F. H. and 1Ibrahim, B. M.
Download Manuscript (68.5 KB)
Abstract: The study was conducted to determine the effects of feeding complete diet of Piliostigma thoningii leaves on growth performance and Digestibility of Kano Brown bucks in semi-arid zone of Nigeria in a randomized complete block design (RCBD) experiment. Sixteen Kano Brown bucks were used in the experiment and are divided into four treatment groups of four animals per treatment viz; T1 (0%) as control, T2 (15%), T3 (30%) and T4 (45%) inclusion levels of Piliostigma thoningii leaves. They were fed the diets ad-libitum. The feeding trial lasted for 12 weeks (84 days). Parameters measured and calculated include weight gain, feed intake, ADG, feed efficiency and growth rate as well as nutrient Digestibility.. The results of growth performance showed that, there were no significant difference (P>0.05) in all the parameters evaluated but values obtained in T3 were numerically higher. The results of digestibility showed significant (P<0.05) differences in all the parameters evaluated except ash digestibility. It is concluded that Piliostigma thoningii leaves could be included in complete diets up to 30% for Kano Brown bucks. It is therefore, recommended that 30% of Piliostigma thoningii could be included in complete ration for Kano Brown bucks.

EFFECT OF GRADED LEVELS OF DIETARY MORINGA OLEIFERA LEAF MEAL ON GROWTH PERFORMANCE AND CARCASS CHARACTERISTICS OF JAPANESE QUAILS

1Igbosanu, A.O., and 1Otufale, G.A.
Download Manuscript (83.5 KB)
Abstract: This experiment was conducted to investigate the effect of graded levels of dietary Moringa oleifera leaf meal on the growth performance and carcass yield characteristics of Japanese quails. Forty eight (48) two-week old Japanese quail chicks were used for the experiment. Birds were allotted to four dietary treatments of twelve quails each which were further replicated twice in a completely randomized design. Four experimental diets were formulated such that diet 1 which served as the control contained no Moringa oleifera leaf meal. Diets 2, 3 and 4 contained 4%, 8% and 12% Moringa oleifera leaf meal. The experimental diets and portable water were supplied ad-libitum throughout the experimental period of 35 days. The dietary treatments were iso-nitrogenous. Results showed that treatment effects on average weight gain, feed conversion ratio, plucked weight, eviscerated weight, dressed weight, head weight, shank weight, gizzard with lining and without lining weights and heart weight were not significant (P > 0.05), while the mean weekly feed intake and live weight of experimental birds were significantly (P < 0.05) enhanced by the control diet and significantly lowered by Moringa oleifera leaf meal inclusion. The Neck weights of the experimental birds were significantly enhanced at 4% level of Moringa oleifera leaf meal supplementation which was similar to the control. Dietary supplementation of Moringa oleifera leaf meal at 4% level compared favourably with the control diet hence, Moringa oleifera leaf meal can be supplemented in the diets of Japanese quails at 4% level for optimal performance and carcass yield.

Genotypic Evaluation of Groundnut (arachis hypogeae L.) Yield-related Traits in Response to Foliar Application of Liquid Organic Fertilizer

1Amira Musa SA’AD, 2*Shamsu Ado ZAKARI, 1Umar ADAMU, and 3Ali Ibrahim SALE
Download Manuscript (156.0 KB)
Abstract: Indiscriminate use of chemical fertilizers has degraded soil quality and reduced groundnut production by depleting nutrients, disrupting microbial balance, and causing soil acidification. Understanding how organic alternatives can enhance growth and yield, reduce chemical fertilizer dependence, and promote sustainable farming is therefore essential.. This study examined the effects of liquid organic fertilizer (LOF) on the growth and yield of two groundnut varieties, SAMNUT24 and SAMNUT25, using four LOF concentrations: 4.7 ml, 6.5 ml, 8.28 ml, and a control (0 ml). The results indicate that LOF and variety significantly influenced leaf number, with SAMNUT25 showing the highest leaf number at 8.28 ml in 2018 and 2019, while SAMNUT24 peaked at 4.7 ml. Both varieties exhibited increased branch number and leaf area with higher LOF concentrations. Total grain yield and yield components were significantly affected by LOF and variety, with the highest 100-seed weight observed at 8.28 ml for both varieties. Control treatments yielded the lowest seed weight. Total pod weight was highest at 8.28 ml for both varieties and decreased with lower LOF concentrations. Positive correlations were found between 100-seed weight, total pod weight, and total grain yield, with SAMNUT25 showing a stronger positive relationship to LOF than SAMNUT24. This study demonstrates that LOF significantly influences groundnut growth and yield, with varying responses among different varieties, providing valuable insights for agricultural practices.

A CAUSALITY ANALYSIS OF CLIMATE CHANGE ON MAIZE PRODUCTION IN KATSINA STATE, NIGERIA.

Umar Sakinatu Garba1, Hussaini Yusuf Ibrahim2, Junaidu Musa3 and Bello Nasiru Abdullahi4
Download Manuscript (103.0 KB)
Abstract: The study examined the causality analysis of climate change on maize production in Katsina State. Time series data covering 1990 to 2020 was used. The analytical tools that were used include descriptive statistics, ARDL model, Toda-Yamamoto granger causality model, variance decomposition and impulse response function. The average values of maize yield (MPY), rainfall (RF), and temperature (TR) were 1.4%, 80.4%, and 26.7%, respectively. ARDL model result showed that there was no long-term relationship, but there was a short-term relationship between climate change and maize production. The outcome of short-run indicated that the model fits the data, with R-square and adjusted R-square values of 80% and 60%, respectively. The result suggests that the current(ΔRF) and first lag (ΔRF (-1)) of Rainfall have a positive impact (0.004824) and (0.003381) and are statistically insignificant at 5% and 1% levels in the short- run. The result implies that the coefficient result suggests that if RF increases by 1%, the MPY will increase in the short term by 0.005% in the short run. The result for Toda Yamamoto causality test showed that the null hypothesis (rainfall does not granger cause maize production and temperature does not granger cause maize production), can be accepted as their probability values (0.4619) and (0.4465) respectively, are greater than (1%, 5% and 10%). From the panel MPY graph, it can be seen that a 1% change in rainfall has a beneficial effect on maize output throughout the periods, however a 1% change in temperature only has a negative effect on maize production during period 2. This outcome suggests that the rain had a beneficial effect on agricultural productivity.

ASSESSMENT OF JOB SATISFACTION AMONG EXTENSION WORKERS’ IN AGRICULTURAL DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME ZONES OF KATSINA STATE, NIGERIA

Yusuf, A.U.1 Mamman, M1. and Adejare T. G.1
Download Manuscript (492.5 KB)
Abstract: The study assesses the job satisfaction among extension workers in agricultural development programme zones of Katsina State, Nigeria using multi-stage sampling techniques to select 75 agricultural extension workers across the three ADP zones of the state. Data were collected using structured questionnaires. The data collected were then analysed using descriptive statistics such as frequency counts, percentage and mean. Similarly, Pearson product moment correlation analysis was used for the inferential components. The findings showed that there was high level of job satisfaction among agricultural extension workers with their job content ( QUOTE =3.82**) and organizational policies ( QUOTE =3.60**), very high level of job satisfaction with their interpersonal relationship ( QUOTE =4.88***) and moderate level of job satisfaction with their condition of service ( QUOTE =3.15*) and working environment ( QUOTE =2.88*). The grand mean score of 3.526** indicated that extension workers had an overall high level of job satisfaction. Three major factors; interpersonal relationship, organizational policies and staff condition of service were found to be strong predictors of job satisfaction. Thus, the study recommended that the Katsina state government should employ more extension staff, pay them good salary and involve them in decision making as well as planning of extension programmes for effective agricultural production. Also, regular promotion and payment of field allowances should be sustained through increased government budgetary allocation and funding of Agricultural Development Programmes.
